# Tomato Growth Stages and Cultivation

**Crop**: Tomato (*Solanum lycopersicum*)
**Topic**: Cultivation, growth stages and water use

## Growth stages and their length
FAO Irrigation and Drainage Paper 56 splits the field tomato season after transplanting into four stages. Its example for an arid region is 30 days initial, 40 days development, 40 days mid-season and 25 days late season (135 days in total). Other regions in the same table range from about 135 to 180 days.

This project records five practical stages: seedling, vegetative, flowering, fruit development and maturity.

## Water use by stage
- FAO-56 crop coefficients (Kc) for tomato: about 0.6 at the initial stage, 1.15 at mid-season and 0.70 to 0.90 at the end of the season. Water use peaks during flowering and fruit growth.
- FAO reports total water requirements of about 400 to 600 mm for a field crop of 90 to 120 days after transplanting, depending on climate.
- Water uptake starts to fall short once more than about 40 percent of the available soil water has been used (depletion fraction p = 0.4).
- Tomato is most sensitive to water shortage during and right after transplanting, and during flowering and fruit formation. Water shortage at flowering causes flower drop.
- Long dry spells followed by heavy watering cause fruit cracking. Steady watering also reduces blossom-end rot.

## Cultivation practices that lower disease pressure
- Stake or cage plants and prune to open the canopy, so leaves dry quickly after dew or rain.
- Space plants for air movement, especially in greenhouses and high tunnels, where humidity builds up.
- Water at the base (drip or furrow) rather than overhead; overhead watering spreads spores and keeps leaves wet.
- Mulch around the base to stop soil splashing onto lower leaves.
- Remove crop debris at the end of the season and rotate away from tomato, potato, pepper and eggplant.
