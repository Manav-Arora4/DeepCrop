# Tomato Septoria Leaf Spot — Disease Management Guide

**Crop**: Tomato (*Solanum lycopersicum*)  
**Disease**: Septoria Leaf Spot  
**Pathogen**: *Septoria lycopersici* (Fungal)  
**Topic**: Plant Pathology & Foliar Disease  

---

## 1. Etiology & Biology
*Septoria lycopersici* is a widespread foliar pathogen overwintering on solanaceous crop debris and nightshade weeds. It produces pycnidia (tiny black fruiting bodies) embedded within circular necrotic lesions. Rain splashes conidia upward from soil onto lower leaves.

## 2. Symptom Identification
- **Leaves**: Numerous small (2–4 mm), circular spots with dark brown margins and sunken tan, beige, or off-white centers.
- **Diagnostic Marker**: Under a hand lens, tiny black specks (pycnidia) are clearly visible within the pale center of mature lesions (distinguishing Septoria from Early Blight and Bacterial Spot).
- **Defoliation**: Progressive upward spread from oldest leaves upward, leading to severe bottom-up defoliation.

## 3. Favorable Conditions
- Temperatures of 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- Prolonged periods of wet leaves (dew or rainfall >8 hours) and high relative humidity.

## 4. Control Strategies
1. **Soil Barrier**: Mulch thoroughly with straw or plastic to suppress spore splash-up from soil.
2. **Bottom Pruning**: Prune the lowest 30–45 cm of leaves once the canopy is established to eliminate the initial splash zone.
3. **Crop Rotation**: Minimum 2-year rotation away from tomato, eggplant, and potato.
4. **Fungicides**: Chlorothalonil, Mancozeb, Copper hydroxide, or Pyraclostrobin applied preventatively when lower leaves are scouted positive.
