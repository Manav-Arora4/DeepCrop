# Tomato Leaf Mold — Disease Management Guide

**Crop**: Tomato (*Solanum lycopersicum*)  
**Disease**: Leaf Mold  
**Pathogen**: *Passalora fulva* (syn. *Fulvia fulva*, *Cladosporium fulvum*) (Fungal)  
**Topic**: Greenhouse & High-Tunnel Pathology  

---

## 1. Disease Etiology & Biology
Leaf mold is a fungal disease particularly prevalent in high tunnels, greenhouses, and humid sheltered microclimates with stagnant airflow. Conidia survive for months on crop residue, wooden greenhouse structures, and twine.

## 2. Symptom Identification
- **Upper Leaf Surface**: Pale green to bright chlorotic yellow patches with indefinite margins.
- **Lower Leaf Surface**: Corresponding to yellow patches above, a dense, velvety olive-green to brown or purplish mold forms as the fungus sporulates through stomata.
- **Progression**: Older leaves are infected first; severely diseased leaves curl, wither, and drop prematurely, causing defoliation and exposing fruit to sunscald.

## 3. Favorable Environmental Conditions
- **Relative Humidity**: Sustained RH >85% is required for sporulation and spore germination. At RH <85%, infection rates drop drastically.
- **Temperature**: Moderate temperatures between 21°C and 24°C (70°F to 75°F).

## 4. Control & Management
1. **Humidity Management (Primary Control)**:
   - Heat and ventilate greenhouses simultaneously at dawn and dusk to expel humid air.
   - Run horizontal airflow (HAF) fans continuously to prevent boundary-layer humidity pockets.
   - Maintain minimum plant spacing of 45–60 cm.
2. **Resistant Cultivars**: Many modern greenhouse varieties contain Cf-resistance genes (Cf-2, Cf-4, Cf-9).
3. **Fungicide Treatments**: Copper fungicides, Chlorothalonil, Cyazofamid, or Boscalid if relative humidity cannot be brought below 85%.
