# Tomato Yellow Leaf Curl Virus (TYLCV) — Disease Management Guide

**Crop**: Tomato (*Solanum lycopersicum*)  
**Disease**: Tomato Yellow Leaf Curl Virus (TYLCV)  
**Virus Genus**: *Begomovirus* (Geminiviridae)  
**Vector**: Silverleaf Whitefly (*Bemisia tabaci*)  
**Topic**: Plant Virology & Vector Entomology  

---

## 1. Etiology & Vector Dynamics
TYLCV is a circular single-stranded DNA plant begomovirus. It is **not** seed-transmitted and **not** mechanically transmitted by touch or pruning tools. It is transmitted in a persistent, circulative manner exclusively by the silverleaf whitefly (*Bemisia tabaci* / *Bemisia argentifolii* complex). A whitefly can acquire the virus in 15–30 minutes of feeding and remain viruliferous for weeks.

## 2. Symptom Identification
- **Leaves**: Severe upward curling ("cupping") of leaf margins, reduced leaf blade size (lamina reduction), and marked interveinal chlorosis (yellowing) of young terminal shoots.
- **Plant Growth**: Severe stunting and bushy, erect appearance due to shortened internodes.
- **Fruit Production**: Dramatic flower abortion. If plants are infected before flowering, yield loss approaches 100%.

## 3. Vector Environmental Conditions
- Whitefly populations surge under hot, dry conditions (27°C to 35°C).

## 4. Control Protocols
1. **Host Plant Resistance (Core Strategy)**: Plant hybrid varieties with Ty-resistance genes (e.g., Ty-1, Ty-2, Ty-3, Ty-5). Examples: 'Tygress', 'Charger', 'Sakura'.
2. **Physical Barriers**:
   - Install 50-mesh insect-proof screening on greenhouse vents.
   - Use silver reflective (metalized) mulches on planting beds to deter incoming whiteflies.
3. **Whitefly Chemical Management**:
   - Systemic neonicotinoids (Imidacloprid, Thiamethoxam) at transplant or via drip chemigation.
   - Diamides (Cyantraniliprole) or insect growth regulators (Pyriproxyfen, Buprofezin).
4. **Sanitation & Rogueing**: Immediately rogue (uproot and bag) symptomatic infected plants to prevent them serving as virus reservoirs.
