# Two-Spotted Spider Mite — Pest Management Guide

**Crop**: Tomato (*Solanum lycopersicum*)  
**Pest**: Two-Spotted Spider Mite (*Tetranychus urticae*)  
**Class**: Arachnida (Arthropod Pest — Not a Microorganism)  
**Topic**: Agricultural Entomology & Acarology  

---

## 1. Biology & Life Cycle
*Tetranychus urticae* is a microscopic acarine pest (0.4 mm long) with two dark lateral spots. Under hot, dry conditions, their generation time can be as short as 5 to 7 days, allowing explosive population growth. They feed by piercing epidermal cells with stylets and sucking out chlorophyll and cell contents.

## 2. Damage Identification
- **Foliage**: Fine, pale yellow or white stippling (pinpoint speckling) on upper leaf surfaces.
- **Webbing**: Silken webbing visible on leaf undersides, growing tips, and flower clusters in advanced infestations.
- **Severe Infestation**: Leaves turn yellow, then bronze or gray, dry out completely ("burn" appearance), and drop prematurely.

## 3. Favorable Environmental Conditions
- **Temperature**: Hot weather (>30°C / 86°F).
- **Humidity**: Low relative humidity (<40%). Spider mites thrive in hot, dry, dusty microclimates.
- **Water Stress**: Drought-stressed tomato plants are significantly more vulnerable due to altered amino acid and sugar concentrations in plant sap.

## 4. Integrated Pest Management (IPM)
1. **Maintain Plant Hydration**: Keep soil moisture within the optimal range (40–70%) to avoid drought-induced vulnerability.
2. **Dust Control**: Wet down field roadways or green pathways; dust disrupts predatory mite movement.
3. **Biological Control**:
   - Release predatory mites: *Phytoseiulus persimilis* (effective at high humidity) or *Neoseiulus californicus* (effective across wider humidity/temp ranges).
   - Encourage ladybird beetles (*Stethorus punctillum*) and predatory thrips.
4. **Biorational Sprays**: Insecticidal soaps, potassium salts of fatty acids, horticultural oils, or neem oil extract (Azadirachtin). Ensure thorough undersurface leaf coverage.
5. **Acaricides (Chemicals)**: Bifenazate, Abamectin, Spiromesifen, or Etoxazole. Rotate chemical classes to prevent rapid acaricide resistance.
