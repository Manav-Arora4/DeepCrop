# Tomato Late Blight — Disease Management Guide

**Crop**: Tomato (*Solanum lycopersicum*)  
**Disease**: Late Blight  
**Pathogen**: *Phytophthora infestans* (Oomycete / Water Mold)  
**Topic**: High-Urgency Plant Pathology  

---

## 1. Disease Etiology & Biology
Late blight is caused by the oomycete *Phytophthora infestans*, historically famous for causing the 1840s Irish Potato Famine. Unlike true fungi, oomycetes produce motile biflagellate zoospores that swim in free water films on leaf surfaces. Late blight is extremely destructive and can decimate entire tomato fields within 7–14 days of first infection under conducive weather.

## 2. Symptom Identification
- **Leaves**: Large, irregular, pale green to olive-colored water-soaked spots that rapidly enlarge and turn dark brown or purplish-black. Under humid conditions, a delicate, white fungal-like growth (sporangia) develops on the abaxial (underside) leaf surface along the lesion margins.
- **Stems & Petioles**: Dark brown to black greasy-looking lesions that can cause entire shoots to collapse and wilt abruptly.
- **Fruit**: Large, firm, greasy olive-brown or golden-brown lesions with an uneven, bumpy surface. Fruit rot remains firm unless secondary soft-rot bacteria invade.

## 3. Favorable Environmental Conditions
- **Temperature**: Cool to moderate temperatures: 15°C to 22°C (60°F to 72°F). Night temperatures of 10°C–15°C followed by moderate day temperatures are ideal.
- **Moisture**: Relative humidity >90% and continuous leaf wetness for 8 to 12 hours. Fog, heavy dew, or prolonged light rain create explosive epidemic conditions.

## 4. Integrated Pest Management (IPM)

### Immediate Action & Cultural Control
1. **Quarantine & Eradication**: Immediately bag and remove severely infected plants during dry midday periods to prevent spore plumes. Do not compost infected debris.
2. **Resistant Cultivars**: Plant cultivars possessing Ph-2 and Ph-3 resistance genes (e.g., 'Defiant PhR', 'Mountain Merit', 'Iron Lady').
3. **Airflow Optimization**: Maintain wide in-row spacing and eliminate weeds to facilitate fast canopy drying.

### Chemical & Fungicide Intervention
- **Preventative**: Protectant fungicides (Chlorothalonil, Mancozeb, Copper hydroxide) must be present on leaf tissue *before* sporulation events.
- **Curative / Translaminar**: Fluopicolide, Cyazofamid, Mandipropamid, Cymoxanil, or Dimethomorph applied with an appropriate surfactant.
- Rotate FRAC mode-of-action codes rigorously.

## 5. Monitoring & Forecasting
Monitor local agricultural extension Late Blight alerts (such as USAblight or Blightwatch). When favorable cool, damp conditions are forecast, implement protective sprays on a tight 5-day cycle.
