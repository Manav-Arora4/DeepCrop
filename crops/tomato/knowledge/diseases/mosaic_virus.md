# Tomato Mosaic Virus (ToMV) — Disease Management Guide

**Crop**: Tomato (*Solanum lycopersicum*)  
**Disease**: Tomato Mosaic Virus (ToMV) / Tobacco Mosaic Virus (TMV)  
**Virus Genus**: *Tobamovirus*  
**Transmission**: Mechanical (Contact) & Seed-Borne  
**Topic**: Plant Virology & Hygiene Protocols  

---

## 1. Etiology & Biology
ToMV is a highly stable rod-shaped positive-sense ssRNA virus. Unlike insect-vectored viruses, tobamoviruses are exceptionally persistent, resisting heat up to 90°C and surviving for years in dry crop debris, on stakes, tools, greenhouse structures, and in tobacco products. Transmission occurs mechanically via worker hands, pruning shears, grafting knives, and seed coats.

## 2. Symptom Identification
- **Leaves**: Light and dark green mosaic or mottled pattern on leaves. Foliage may become distorted, wrinkled, blistered, or narrow into a "fern-leaf" or "shoestring" shape.
- **Plant**: Mild to moderate stunting.
- **Fruit**: Uneven ripening, internal brown necrosis (vascular browning), and surface yellow blotching.

## 3. Control & Strict Sanitation Protocols
1. **Resistant Cultivars**: Use varieties possessing the *Tm-2* or *Tm-2²* resistance alleles.
2. **Decontamination Protocol**:
   - Disinfect pruning tools frequently by dipping in a 20% non-fat dry milk solution or 10% trisodium phosphate (TSP) solution. (Milk proteins bind and neutralize virus coat proteins).
   - Require workers to wash hands with soap and water before handling plants.
   - Prohibit smoking, chewing, or handling tobacco products in or near tomato growing areas.
3. **Seed Sanitation**: Treat seeds with 10% trisodium phosphate (TSP) for 30 minutes or dry heat (70°C for 2–4 days).
4. **Immediate Disposal**: Remove infected plants along with their immediate root system and bag them on site; do not drag through the crop canopy.
