# Tomato Bacterial Spot — Disease Management Guide

**Crop**: Tomato (*Solanum lycopersicum*)  
**Disease**: Bacterial Spot  
**Pathogen**: *Xanthomonas perforans*, *Xanthomonas vesicatoria*, *X. euvesicatoria*, *X. gardneri* (Bacterial)  
**Topic**: Plant Pathology & Bacteriology  

---

## 1. Disease Etiology & Biology
Bacterial spot is caused by four species of *Xanthomonas*. Bacteria enter plants through natural openings (stomata, hydathodes) or wounds caused by wind-driven sand, insect feeding, pruning, or hail. Bacteria survive on infected crop residue, seed coats, volunteers, and solanaceous weeds.

## 2. Symptom Identification
- **Leaves**: Small (2–3 mm), dark brown to black, water-soaked angular spots, often surrounded by a distinct yellow halo. As spots multiply, leaf margins become blighted, dry, and tear ("shot-hole" or ragged appearance).
- **Stems**: Elongated dark streaks or pustules.
- **Fruit**: Most diagnostic symptom — small, raised, brown blister-like spots that become scab-like, rough, and cracked, rendering fruit unmarketable.

## 3. Favorable Environmental Conditions
- **Temperature**: Warm to hot temperatures (25°C to 30°C / 77°F to 86°F).
- **Moisture**: High relative humidity (>80%) and driving rain or overhead irrigation that facilitates bacterial splash dispersal.

## 4. Management & Control Protocols
1. **Certified Disease-Free Seed**: Use hot-water treated (50°C for 25 minutes) or hydrochloric acid-treated seed.
2. **Avoid Overhead Irrigation**: Wet leaves provide the liquid film bacteria need for motility and penetration.
3. **Never Work Wet Foliage**: Farm workers and machinery easily spread bacteria between plants when foliage is damp with dew or rain.
4. **Bactericides**: Fixed copper formulations combined with Mancozeb (to overcome copper-tolerant bacterial strains) or Actigard (acibenzolar-S-methyl) systemic acquired resistance inducer.
5. **Bacteriophages**: In organic production, specific bacteriophage sprays (*AgriPhage*) targeted against *Xanthomonas* provide biocontrol.
