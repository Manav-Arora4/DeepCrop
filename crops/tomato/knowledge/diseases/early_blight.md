# Tomato Early Blight — Disease Management Guide

**Crop**: Tomato (*Solanum lycopersicum*)  
**Disease**: Early Blight  
**Pathogen**: *Alternaria solani* (Fungal)  
**Topic**: Plant Pathology & Disease Management  

---

## 1. Disease Etiology & Biology
Early blight is caused by the necrotrophic fungus *Alternaria solani*. It survives between seasons in infected plant debris, volunteer solanaceous plants, and alternative weed hosts (such as black nightshade). Conidia are dispersed primarily by wind, rain splashing, overhead irrigation, and mechanical tools.

## 2. Symptom Identification
- **Leaves**: Initial symptoms appear on older, lower foliage as small, dark brown to black spots. As lesions enlarge (up to 1.5 cm), they develop characteristic concentric ridges resembling a "target" pattern with a chlorotic (yellow) halo surrounding the necrotic tissue.
- **Stems**: Dark, slightly sunken, concentric ring lesions can girdle seedlings at soil level (collar rot) or older stems.
- **Fruit**: Sunken, leathery dark brown/black lesions near the stem end, typically showing concentric zones.

## 3. Favorable Environmental Conditions
- **Temperature**: 24°C to 29°C (75°F to 84°F) is optimal for sporulation and infection.
- **Moisture**: High relative humidity (>85%) combined with prolonged leaf wetness (6+ hours from dew, rain, or overhead watering).
- **Host Predisposition**: Heavy fruit loads, nitrogen/potassium nutritional stress, or drought stress accelerate disease onset.

## 4. Integrated Pest Management (IPM)

### Cultural & Physical Controls
1. **Sanitation**: Remove and destroy infected lower leaves upon early detection to reduce inoculum load.
2. **Crop Rotation**: Rotate out of Solanaceae (tomato, potato, eggplant, pepper) for at least 3 years.
3. **Canopy Management**: Stake, prune, and space plants generously to promote rapid air drying of foliage.
4. **Irrigation Protocol**: Transition exclusively to drip or furrow irrigation. Never use overhead sprinklers late in the day.
5. **Mulching**: Apply organic or plastic mulch around plant bases to prevent soil-splash dispersal of fungal spores.

### Biological & Organic Controls
- *Bacillus subtilis* (e.g., strain QST 713) preventive biofungicide foliar sprays.
- Copper-based formulations (copper hydroxide / copper octanoate) applied at first sign of disease.

### Chemical Controls (Advisory Notice)
- Protectant fungicides: Chlorothalonil or Mancozeb applied preventatively prior to canopy closure.
- Systemic / Translaminar options: Azoxystrobin, Difenoconazole, or Boscalid rotation to prevent fungicide resistance.
- Always observe pre-harvest intervals (PHI) and local regulatory labels.

## 5. Monitoring & Decision Thresholds
Scout the lower canopy weekly starting at transplanting. If 5% of lower leaves display early blight target lesions and relative humidity exceeds 75%, trigger immediate fungicide application and pruning.
