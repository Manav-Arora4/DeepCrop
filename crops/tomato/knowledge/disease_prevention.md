# Preventing Tomato Foliar Diseases

**Crop**: Tomato (*Solanum lycopersicum*)
**Topic**: Disease prevention (integrated pest management)

## Conditions that favour disease
- Most fungal and bacterial leaf diseases need water on the leaf surface to infect. Long periods of leaf wetness from dew, rain or overhead watering raise risk.
- Early blight is favoured by warm weather (about 24 to 29 °C) with high humidity and long leaf wetness. The same conditions favour Septoria leaf spot.
- Late blight is favoured by cool, wet weather, and its spores spread easily by wind. Sprinkler irrigation favours late blight.
- Leaf mold is driven by relative humidity above about 85 percent, mainly in greenhouses and high tunnels, with an optimum near 22 to 24 °C.
- Target spot is favoured by moderate temperatures of about 21 to 27 °C (70 to 80 °F).
- Spider mites are the opposite: they multiply quickly in warm, dry conditions.
- Tomato yellow leaf curl virus is spread by whiteflies; tomato mosaic virus spreads by contact and survives for years in dry plant debris.

## Prevention practices
1. **Keep leaves dry.** Water at the base, early in the day, and avoid overhead sprinklers.
2. **Improve air movement.** Stake, prune and space plants. In greenhouses, ventilate when humidity rises above 85 percent.
3. **Remove sources of infection.** Pick off infected lower leaves early, remove crop debris after harvest, and clear volunteer tomatoes, potatoes and nightshade weeds.
4. **Rotate crops.** Avoid planting tomato family crops in the same place in consecutive seasons.
5. **Use resistant or tolerant varieties** where they exist. Early blight tolerance is available in several varieties; Septoria resistance is less common; no commercial varieties resist target spot.
6. **Manage vectors.** Control whiteflies to limit yellow leaf curl virus, and wash hands and tools to limit mosaic virus spread.
7. **Scout regularly.** Check lower leaves at least weekly, and look under leaves for mites and whiteflies. Early detection is the key to control.
8. **Use fungicides only as a protective measure,** following local extension advice, and rotate products by FRAC code to avoid resistance.
