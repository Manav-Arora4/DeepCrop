# Tomato Irrigation & Soil Moisture Management Guide

**Crop**: Tomato (*Solanum lycopersicum*)  
**Topic**: Precision Water Management & Sensor Integration  

---

## 1. Crop Water Requirements
Tomatoes require 400–650 mm (16–26 inches) of water throughout their lifecycle, depending on temperature, solar radiation, and wind. Water demand peaks sharply during the flowering and fruit-expansion phases.

| Growth Stage | Duration (Days) | Water Demand (mm/week) | Key Sensitivity |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Seedling / Establishment** | 0–14 | 15–25 | Shallow rooting; avoid desiccation |
| **Vegetative** | 14–35 | 25–35 | Building leaf area; steady supply |
| **Flowering & Fruit Set** | 35–60 | 35–50 | **Critical**: Moisture stress causes flower abortion |
| **Fruit Development** | 60–90 | 45–60 | Moisture fluctuation causes fruit cracking & blossom end rot |
| **Ripening / Maturity** | 90–120 | 20–30 | Reduce water to concentrate sugars and brix |

---

## 2. Soil Moisture Interpretation & Thresholds
Volumetric Water Content (VWC %) measured by capacitive soil moisture probes:

- **< 30% (LOW / Water Deficit)**: 
  - Stomatal closure occurs, reducing photosynthesis.
  - Calcium transport is disrupted, leading directly to Blossom End Rot (BER).
  - *Action*: Trigger drip irrigation cycle immediately.
- **30% – 70% (OPTIMAL)**:
  - Ideal balance between plant-available water and soil pore oxygenation.
  - *Action*: Maintain current scheduling.
- **> 70% (HIGH / Waterlogged)**:
  - Soil oxygen depletion (hypoxia) occurs within 24 hours.
  - Favors root pathogens (*Pythium*, *Phytophthora nicotianae*, *Fusarium oxysporum*).
  - *Action*: Cease irrigation immediately and inspect field drainage channels.

---

## 3. Soil pH & Nutrient Bioavailability
Optimal range: **6.0 to 6.8**
- **pH < 5.5 (Acidic)**: Aluminum and manganese toxicity risk; phosphorus and calcium binding.
- **pH > 7.2 (Alkaline)**: Severe micronutrient lockout (Iron, Zinc, Manganese chlorosis).
